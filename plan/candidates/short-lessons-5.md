## wonder (6 needed)

6. ★ Mon. **tides: Tides, and the Bay of Fundy** (science). Week 4's 20. The Moon's pull lifts the ocean into a bulge,
   and as the Earth turns most coasts get two high and two low tides a day; near a full moon the Sun and Moon pull in
   line and the tides are biggest. In Canada's Bay of Fundy the water can rise more than 50 feet.
   `commons-nasa-fundy-tides`: *Fundy High & Low tide.jpg*, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Fundy_High_%26_Low_tide.jpg,
   public domain (NASA). Small (540 by 720). None.
7. ★ Wed. **archimedes-lever: A lever, and Archimedes moving the Earth** (science; new). A lever is a bar that turns on
   a point, the fulcrum: push the long end a long way and the short end lifts a heavy load a little, which is how a
   seesaw, a crowbar and a bottle opener work. Archimedes is said to have boasted, "Give me a place to stand and I will
   move the Earth." `commons-archimedes-lever-1824`: *Archimedes lever (Small).jpg*,
   https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Archimedes_lever_(Small).jpg, public domain (Mechanics Magazine, London,
   1824). None.
8. ★ Thu. **harvey-circulation: Circulation, and the blood that goes around** (science; new). The heart is a pump: the
   same blood goes out through the arteries and back through the veins, around and around. William Harvey showed it
   in 1628: with a band tied around the arm, the veins swell below it, and little valves in them let blood go only
   toward the heart. A child can find a pulse in a wrist. `commons-harvey-arm-plates-1628`: the four figures of the arm from the 1628 book (Bibliothèque nationale de France),
   https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Illustrations_de_Exercitatio_anatomica_de_motu_cordis_et_sanguinis_in_animalibus..._-_(Non_identifi%C3%A9)_;_William_Harvey,_aut._du_texte_-_btv1b2100015j_(2_of_2).jpg,
   public domain (taken at step 2 in place of *William Harvey (1578-1657) Venenbild.jpg*, whose credit read "Unknown
   authorUnknown author, Unknown date"). None (his experiments on animals are left out).
9. ★ Fri. **bioluminescence: Bioluminescence, light made by living things** (science; new). Fireflies, glowing
   jellies and deep-sea fish make light with a chemical reaction inside their bodies, cold light with almost no heat;
   in the dark sea most animals glow, to find a mate, to lure food or to startle a hunter. **A Hawaiʻi line, your
   call:** the Hawaiian bobtail squid, a thumb-sized squid of Oʻahu's shallow sand flats, carries glowing bacteria
   in its belly and dims or brightens them to match the moonlight from above, so it casts no shadow.
   `commons-widder-euplokamis-glow`: *Bioluminescence emitted by comb jelly of genus Euplokamis.jpg*,
   https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Bioluminescence_emitted_by_comb_jelly_of_genus_Euplokamis.jpg, public
   domain (F. Widder, 1999, as the source names the photographer). `commons-noaa-glowing-coral`: *Expl2337 - Flickr - NOAA Photo Library.jpg*,
   https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Expl2337_-_Flickr_-_NOAA_Photo_Library.jpg, public domain (NOAA, 2009).
   None.
10. ★ Sat. **muybridge-horse: Stop-motion photography, and the horse with all four hooves off the ground** (science).
    Week 4's 21. In 1878 Muybridge set up a row of cameras that fired one after another as a horse galloped past, and
    showed that all four hooves leave the ground at once, tucked under the body. `commons-muybridge-horse-1878`: *The
    Horse in Motion high res.jpg*, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_Horse_in_Motion_high_res.jpg, public
    domain. None.
11. ★ Sun. **compass-north: A magnetic field, and why a compass points north** (science). Week 3's 16. The Earth is a
    giant magnet and a compass needle a small one that lines up with it; in 1600 William Gilbert showed it with a round

23. ★ Mon. **roman-numerals: Roman numerals, and the clock that writes four as IIII** (math). Week 3's 39. I, V, X, L

24. ★ Tue. **truss-triangles: A triangle keeps its shape, and the truss bridge** (math; new). Push on a square of four

26. ★ Thu. **cube-net: A net, the flat shape that folds into a cube** (math; new). A cube has six square faces, and
