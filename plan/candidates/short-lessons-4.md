## wonder (7 needed)

12. ★ Mon. **saturn-rings: A planet's rings** (science). Saturn's rings are billions of pieces of ice and rock, from
    specks of dust to the size of a house, circling the planet in a band very wide and very thin; in 1610 Galileo's
    telescope showed them as two blobs, like ears. `commons-cassini-saturn-equinox`: *Saturn during Equinox.jpg*,
    https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Saturn_during_Equinox.jpg, public domain (NASA, 2008). None.
13. ★ Tue. **nene: An endangered species, and the nēnē** (science; Hawaiʻi). The nēnē, Hawaiʻi's state bird, is a
    goose found nowhere else, with less webbing on its feet for walking on lava; by the early 1950s about 30 were left
    in the wild, and people bred them and set them free. `commons-usfws-nene-2014`: *Nēnē, Branta sandvicensis, an
    endangered endemic bird in Hawai`i (38848451080).jpg*,
    https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:N%C4%93n%C4%93,_Branta_sandvicensis,_an_endangered_endemic_bird_in_Hawai%60i_(38848451080).jpg,
    public domain (USFWS). None.
14. ★ Wed. **water-cycle: The water cycle** (science). Water evaporates from the ocean, rises, cools into clouds, falls
    as rain, and runs back to the sea, over and over; the same water has gone around for billions of years. (A
    simpler line for the younger: where a puddle goes.) `commons-usgs-water-cycle-2022`: *USGS WaterCycle English
    ONLINE 20221013.png*, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:USGS_WaterCycle_English_ONLINE_20221013.png, public
    domain (USGS). Very large, so its preview is stored. None. ("Billions of years" is point 5's ground; the summary
    will say "again and again" instead.)
15. ★ Thu. **pahoehoe-aa: Pāhoehoe and ʻaʻā, two Hawaiian words the whole world uses** (science; Hawaiʻi). Smooth,
    ropy lava is pāhoehoe and rough, broken lava is ʻaʻā, and geologists everywhere use the Hawaiian words; the same
    lava can start as pāhoehoe and turn into ʻaʻā as it cools and thickens. `commons-usgs-pahoehoe-skylight`: *Molten
    Lava in Pahoehoe Skylight.jpg*, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Molten_Lava_in_Pahoehoe_Skylight.jpg,
    public domain (USGS). `commons-usgs-aa-front`: *Aa large.jpg*, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Aa_large.jpg,
    public domain (USGS). None.
16. ★ Fri. **roman-arch: An arch, and the aqueduct with no mortar** (science). The stones of an arch push against each
    other, and the top one, the keystone, locks them; the Romans built the aqueduct at Segovia, in Spain, of granite
    blocks with no mortar, and it still stands. `commons-loc-segovia-aqueduct-1900`: *Segovia-El Puente
    LCCN90708233.jpg*, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Segovia-El_Puente_LCCN90708233.jpg, public domain
    (Library of Congress photochrom, about 1900). None. (From the first file's "worth a thought": how a bridge holds
    itself up.)
17. ★ Sat. **hee-octopus: An invertebrate, and the heʻe** (science; Hawaiʻi). The heʻe, the day octopus, has no bones,
    so it can squeeze through any gap bigger than its beak; it has three hearts and blue blood, and changes color to
    hide. `commons-noaa-hee-octopus`: *PMNM - He'e Octopus (27551309582).jpg*,
    https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:PMNM_-_He%27e_Octopus_(27551309582).jpg, public domain (NOAA,
    Papahānaumokuākea). None.
18. ★ Sun. **solar-eclipse: A solar eclipse** (science). When the Moon passes exactly between the Sun and the Earth,
    its shadow falls on a strip of the Earth and the day goes dark for a few minutes, showing the Sun's glowing
    corona; the island of Hawaiʻi had a total eclipse on 11 July 1991. `commons-nasa-eclipse-2017`: *2017 Total Solar
    Eclipse (AFRC2017-0233-008).jpg*, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:2017_Total_Solar_Eclipse_(AFRC2017-0233-008).jpg,
    public domain (NASA). None.
19. (h) **seeds-to-hawaii: Seed dispersal** (Hawaiʻi). Week 3's 15. None.
20. **tides: Tides, and the Bay of Fundy** (science). The Moon's pull lifts the ocean into a bulge, and as the Earth
    turns most coasts get two high tides and two low tides a day; in Canada's Bay of Fundy the water can rise more
    than 50 feet. `commons-nasa-fundy-tides`: *Fundy High & Low tide.jpg*,
    https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Fundy_High_%26_Low_tide.jpg, public domain (NASA). Small (540 by 720). None.
21. **muybridge-horse: Stop-motion photography, and the horse with all four hooves off the ground** (science). In
    1878 Eadweard Muybridge set up a row of cameras that fired one after another as a horse galloped past, and showed
    that all four hooves leave the ground at once, tucked under the body. `commons-muybridge-horse-1878`: *The Horse

## puzzle (6 needed)

43. ★ Mon. **konigsberg-bridges: A network, and Euler's seven bridges** (math). Königsberg had seven bridges joining
    two islands and the riverbanks, and people tried to walk across each one exactly once; in 1736 Leonhard Euler
    showed it cannot be done, because every piece of land touches an odd number of bridges.
    `commons-konigsberg-bridges-merian`: *Konigsberg Bridge.png*, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Konigsberg_Bridge.png,
