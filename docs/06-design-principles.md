# Design principles for winning attention

To compete for kids' attention, the tutor borrows the honest hooks (novelty, choice, collecting, social payoff) and leaves out the manipulative ones.

## Use

- **Something new daily.** There's always something they haven't seen yet.
- **Their choice.** They pick what to tap and how deep to go.
- **Collecting.** Explorer stamps for each era, artist or idea they've explored.
- **Their interests first.** Sharks, knights or space are the way in, and broader topics connect from there.
- **Social payoff.** Things to teach a sibling or bring up at dinner.
- **Real difficulty.** Challenge modes for older kids, and puzzles that are actually hard.

## Leave out

- autoplay
- infinite scroll
- streak guilt
- notifications
- outbound links
- ads
- any reward that only exists to make them come back

## Endings on purpose

Each topic ends after 3–4 layers with a question to take to a person. The screen hands kids off to real life.

## House rules help

On a level field, this won't beat YouTube. A simple family rule like "the Wonder Wall comes before other screens" makes it the default.
