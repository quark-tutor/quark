# Visions and evidence to build on

Writers have imagined a personal tutor for every child for 50 years. The best visions and the newest evidence agree on one thing: the machine works when it supports loving adults and makes the child think, and it fails when it replaces either.

## The visions

| Who | Work | Core idea | What we take |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Neal Stephenson | [*The Diamond Age*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Diamond_Age) (1995) | The Young Lady's Illustrated Primer, an interactive book that raises a girl by weaving every lesson into a story about her own life. It works because a real human actor voices it with love. The cheaper mass-produced copies, with no human behind them, raise a uniform, obedient army. Asked later how to build it, he said: "[Kids need to get answers from humans who love them](https://simonwillison.net/2024/Jun/4/a-tip-from-neal-stephenson/)." | Our North Star, and our warning. Keep parents' voices inside the tutor, and never mass-produce one way of thinking. |
| Benjamin Bloom | ["The 2 Sigma Problem"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloom%27s_2_sigma_problem) (1984) | One-on-one tutoring with mastery learning lifted average students above 98% of classroom students. The open problem was making it affordable. | The prize: a tutor for every child. |
| Seymour Papert | [*Mindstorms*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mindstorms_(book)) (1980) | The child should program the computer, not the computer program the child. Kids learn deeply by building things. | Make things, not only consume cards: projects, experiments, building. |
| Alan Kay | [Dynabook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynabook) (1972) | A personal computer for children of all ages, as a medium for thinking and creating | The device is a tool for thinking, not a feed. |
| Isaac Asimov | ["The Fun They Had"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fun_They_Had) (1951) | A future child with a mechanical home teacher envies the old schools, where kids learned together and laughed. | Learning is social. Build in siblings, family and friends. |
| Ivan Illich | [*Deschooling Society*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deschooling_Society) (1971) | "Learning webs" that connect people to resources, mentors and peers, instead of institutions | Connect kids to real people and communities, not only to content. |
| Sugata Mitra | [Hole in the Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hole_in_the_Wall_(computer)) (1999 on) | Groups of curious kids with a computer and a big question teach themselves a surprising amount. | Big questions and self-directed exploration, as on the Wonder Wall. |
| Sal Khan | [*Brave New Words*](https://blog.khanacademy.org/sal-khans-2023-ted-talk-ai-in-the-classroom-can-transform-education/) (2024) | AI can finally deliver Bloom's 2 sigma tutoring. It should ask questions rather than give answers, and parents should see everything. | Socratic by default, full parent visibility. |
| Andrej Karpathy | [Eureka Labs](https://eurekalabs.ai/) (2024) | A human teacher designs the course, and an AI assistant guides each student through it. | The plan is designed by people; the AI delivers it and supports each child. |
| Andy Matuschak | ["How Might We Learn?"](https://andymatuschak.org/hmwl/) (2024) | Chatbot tutors forget the learner and sit in a box apart from real life. Better tutors are woven into a learner's own projects, remember them, and lead into real communities of practice. | Start from each child's interests, and keep a lasting memory of each child. |

## The evidence so far

| Study | Finding | What it means for us |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Harvard physics trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12179260/) (2025) | A well-designed AI tutor beat active in-class learning by roughly 0.7–1.3 standard deviations. The gains came from pre-written solutions and structured steps, not from the prompt alone. | People design the structure. The AI doesn't improvise the curriculum. |
| [World Bank, Nigeria](https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/education/From-chalkboards-to-chatbots-Transforming-learning-in-Nigeria) (2025) | Six weeks of AI-assisted after-school English, with teachers present, produced about 0.3 standard deviations of gains (roughly two years of typical learning). Gains grew with attendance. | Regular routines with adults present matter more than features. |
| [Penn / PNAS high school math](https://chibe.upenn.edu/publications/generative-ai-without-guardrails-can-harm-learning-evidence-from-high-school-mathematics) (2025) | Plain GPT-4 raised practice scores but lowered exam scores by 17%. A version that gave hints instead of answers avoided the harm. | Hints, not answers. Check regularly what kids can do without the AI. |
| [Alpha School review](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/your-review-alpha-school) (2025) | Strong results in school depended on adult guides and motivation systems. The home version showed much weaker growth. | The real product at home is motivation and adult structure. This is our hardest problem. |
| [Haidt and Rausch](https://www.afterbabel.com/p/dont-give-your-child-an-ai-companion) (2025) | AI "companions" built for engagement displace human relationships. | The tutor is never a friend or companion. It's a guide that hands kids back to people. |

## Principles this gives us

- **Humans who love the child stay at the center.** The tutor amplifies parents and never replaces them.
- **Make the child think.** Ask questions and give hints, never just answers. Check what they can do on their own.
- **People design the structure, and the AI delivers it.** That means a long-term plan, a worked sequence and reviewed content.
- **Remember each child and start from their interests.**
- **Make, don't just consume.** Projects, experiments, building and teaching a sibling.
- **No companion, no engagement tricks.** Warmth, yes. Attachment and dependence, no.
- **Many voices, not one mold.** Guard against the mass-produced, uniform kind of Primer.
