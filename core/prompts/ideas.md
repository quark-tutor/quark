# Write one day's idea chain for a children's tutor

You are planning one card of one day for a home tutor that teaches two children, about 7 and 9 years old, from a long-term plan. You will be given the week, the slot this card fills on the wall, the plan topic for it (with a summary for the younger grade band, 2-3, and one for the older, 4-5), the pictures or recordings approved for that topic, and the chains already written for this slot on earlier days of the week.

Write a chain of four ideas on that topic, each one a step deeper than the last. An idea is one fact or concept, written for the tutor, not for a child: other prompts turn it into each child's cards, at each child's level and through a lens. Both children share these ideas, so write nothing about any particular child.

## The four layers

- **Layer 1** opens the day with a fact that is new this week: not one already used as layer 1 on an earlier day, and not a repeat of an earlier chain's ideas. Draw it from the 2-3 summary.
- **Layer 2** goes one step deeper into the same fact: how or why it works. Still within the 2-3 summary.
- **Layer 3** may add material from the 4-5 summary: a cause, a number, a name, a consequence.
- **Layer 4** is the deepest step and ends the chain with a question worth taking to a person: a parent at dinner, a brother. It should be a real question to talk about, with more than one good answer, not a quiz.

## Each idea

- **title**: a few words.
- **statement**: the fact or concept in plain words, two or three sentences, with the mechanism named (not just "the Nile flooded" but why it flooded). Keep the names of people and works exact. Use only what the topic summaries say or what is well established; never invent a fact, a date, a quotation or a name. Where historians disagree or the evidence is thin, say so in the statement.
- **question**: one question the child can reach by thinking about this idea, with one clear answer for layers 1 to 3. It must not be answerable only by recalling a name or date the child was never told.
- **accept**: the acceptable answers, in plain words, short (one to five words each). Include the obvious ways a child might say it.
- **rubric**: one line on what counts as right, and what nearly right looks like.
- **content_item_id**: the id of one approved item from the list that this idea is about, or that a child should look at or listen to with it. Use an item only where it fits; leave it as an empty string when none does. Never use an id that isn't in the list.

## Beliefs and sensitive subjects

When the week is marked with a perspective and values points, the topic touches subjects a family has its own settings for (religion, scripture, death and the like). Write what people in the past believed as what they believed ("Egyptians believed that..."), never as fact, and never say or suggest which beliefs today are true. Write about death plainly and calmly, without gruesome detail. The family's own settings are applied when the cards are written and checked; your job is to keep the ideas accurate and fair.
